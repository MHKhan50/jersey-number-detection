JerseyNet: find jersey numbers in an image

A small detector (4.7 million parameters) trained from scratch, with no ImageNet or COCO weights, draws a box around every jersey number it finds. Everything runs in your browser: the image never leaves your device.

Research demo. The model was trained only on small (128 × 128 px) ice-hockey images. It works best on hockey photos where the number is clearly visible; on other sports, large photos or crowded scenes it will miss numbers and make mistakes. It finds where numbers are; it does not read the digits.

The model

  • JerseyNet: a CenterNet-style detector with a residual backbone and a feature pyramid down to stride 4.
  • Trained from random initialisation on 3,165 images of the public jersey-2 set, with mosaic augmentation, a GIoU box loss and a 384 px input.
  • Test AP50:95 0.314 and AP50 0.593 (this checkpoint); 0.308 ± 0.005 over three seeds.

How it runs

  • The model is exported to ONNX and runs with ONNX Runtime Web (WebAssembly).
  • The image is letterboxed to 384 × 384 exactly as in training; the boxes are decoded from the centre heatmap without NMS.
  • On the test split this app's pipeline reproduces the Python results (AP50:95 0.314).
